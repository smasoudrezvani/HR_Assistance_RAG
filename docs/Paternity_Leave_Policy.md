# Paternity and Partner Leave Policy
**Call360 | Version 1.3 | Effective January 2026**

## Purpose

Call360 recognizes the importance of time for all partners following the birth or adoption of a child. This policy applies to all non-primary caregivers (typically referred to as the partner or co-parent).

## Entitlements by Region

### Netherlands (NL)
- **1 week (5 days) paid partner leave** at 100% — to be taken within the first 4 weeks after birth (called "geboorteverlof" / birth leave)
- **Additional paid parental leave**: the first 9 weeks of the statutory 26-week parental entitlement may be taken in the first year and are paid at 70% (via UWV); the remaining weeks are unpaid

### South Africa (ZA)
- **10 consecutive days** of paid partner leave following the birth of a child
- Must be taken within 1 month of birth
- Employees must have completed at least 13 weeks of employment to qualify

### Remote (Global)
- **1 standard working week (5 days) paid** at 100% of salary
- To be taken within the first month following the birth of the child
- Non-primary caregivers in adoption situations also qualify (see Adoption Leave Policy)

## Notification Requirements

Employees should notify the People & Culture team at least **1 month before** the expected birth date:
- Intended leave start date
- Expected return-to-work date

Notify HR by emailing hr@call360.com and submitting a leave request in PeopleBase.

## Same-Sex Partners and Non-Traditional Families

This policy is fully inclusive. It applies equally to all partners regardless of gender or family structure. For family arrangements not explicitly covered here, please contact HR to discuss your specific situation.

## Non-Primary Caregiver in Adoption

Non-primary caregiver partners in adoption situations are entitled to the same partner leave entitlement as biological parents. See the Adoption Leave Policy for primary caregiver entitlements.

## Return to Work

Managers should schedule a brief check-in with returning employees to ensure a smooth transition back to work, particularly after extended parental leave.
