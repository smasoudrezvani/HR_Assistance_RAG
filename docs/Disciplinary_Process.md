# Disciplinary Process
**Call360 | Version 1.3 | Effective January 2026**

## Purpose

Call360's disciplinary process is designed to address employee conduct or performance issues fairly, consistently, and proportionately. The goal is usually to correct behavior — not to punish.

## Scope

This process applies to issues of misconduct (behavior-related) rather than performance issues. Poor performance is addressed through the Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) process.

## Categories of Misconduct

### Minor Misconduct
Examples: persistent lateness, minor policy violations, careless use of equipment, low-level disrespect

### Serious Misconduct
Examples: repeated minor misconduct, insubordination, unauthorized absence, inappropriate conduct at work events

### Gross Misconduct
Examples that may lead to immediate dismissal without prior warnings:
- Theft or fraud
- Physical assault or serious verbal abuse
- Serious data breach or deliberate violation of security policy
- Sexual harassment
- Being under the influence of alcohol or illegal substances at work
- Willful falsification of records

## The Disciplinary Process

### Step 1: Investigation
Before any disciplinary action, HR will conduct a fair investigation. The employee will be informed of the allegations and given a chance to respond. During investigation, the employee may be placed on paid suspension — this is not a disciplinary action.

### Step 2: Disciplinary Hearing
A formal meeting is held where:
- The allegation is presented
- The employee can share their account and call witnesses
- The employee may be accompanied by a colleague or trade union representative

At least 3 business days' written notice is given before the hearing.

### Step 3: Outcome

Following the hearing, the outcome is communicated in writing within 3 business days. Possible outcomes:

| Outcome | When Used |
|---|---|
| No action | Allegations not substantiated |
| Verbal warning | Minor first-time misconduct |
| First written warning | Moderate misconduct or repeated minor misconduct (valid 6 months) |
| Final written warning | Serious misconduct or repeated written warnings (valid 12 months) |
| Dismissal | Gross misconduct or failure to improve after final written warning |

### Step 4: Appeal

The employee may appeal the outcome within **5 business days** of receiving the written decision. The appeal will be heard by a manager or HR Director not involved in the original hearing.

## Record Keeping

Warnings are placed on the employee's personnel file for the duration specified. After the validity period, warnings are removed from active consideration (though retained for record purposes).
